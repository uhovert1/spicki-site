Islamabad hookah cafés shut by court order as owners appeal. What it means for Belgrade

17 November 2026

In Pakistan's capital every hookah café is closed until the authorities adopt rules for the business. Owners are challenging the decision on appeal, and the court is waiting for the city administration to respond.

What happened

In Islamabad, the capital of Pakistan, hookah cafés have now spent a month under a complete ban. In mid-October the Chief Justice of the Islamabad High Court, Sardar Muhammad Sarfraz Dogar, ordered all shisha venues in the city to close and their no-objection certificates (NOCs) to be withdrawn. As The Express Tribune and The Nation reported, the cafés must stay shut until new rules for the sector are in place.

The ruling caps a long saga. In September the court gave the authorities a month to draft rules, and in a written order dated 12 September, according to Dawn, it barred "coercive measures" against venues operating lawfully. In October the court said that order had been misread as permission for shisha cafés to operate. A government lawyer said draft rules had been prepared and sent for approval, but they had not been adopted at the time of reporting.

Why the court imposed a ban

The case began with a petition seeking action against illegal shisha venues. According to The Nation, police registered 28 hookah-related cases against 119 people between 1 January and 15 July 2026, and another 61 cases between 16 July and 7 September. According to Dawn, police also reported finding teenagers aged 14 and 15 at some cafés. The court asked how permits could have been issued at all when no rules for shisha cafés exist.

Owners appeal

On 4 November café owners filed an appeal against the judge's decision. A two-judge bench, Justices Raja Inaam Ameen Minhas and Ayaz Shaukat, asked the district administration to respond and adjourned without suspending the ban. The owners, according to The Nation, say each of them invested more than 40 million Pakistani rupees on the strength of their permits, and that the ban targets shisha while cigarettes remain on sale. Meanwhile the administration, according to ICCI, ordered daily checks across the city. On 13 November ProPakistani reported that owners are still challenging the closure in court. There is no final decision yet.

What it means for Serbia and Belgrade

Nothing changes for Serbia: the Pakistani ruling applies only in Islamabad, and hookah lounges in Belgrade operate under Serbian law. The story is interesting for another reason: it shows what happens when a shisha business runs for years without clear rules. Permits get issued, money gets invested, and then a single ruling closes everything until the state writes the regulations.

For guests the practical takeaway is simple: if you are travelling to Pakistan, bear in mind that hookah cafés in Islamabad are currently closed. Rules differ widely elsewhere too, so it is worth checking them before a trip. Hookah is for adults only, and that rule applies everywhere.

FAQ

Are hookah cafés open in Islamabad?

No. Since mid-October 2026 all shisha cafés have been closed by order of the Islamabad High Court until new rules are adopted.

Has Islamabad's hookah café ban been lifted?

No. Owners appealed on 4 November, but the court did not suspend the ban and asked the administration to respond.

No. It applies only in Islamabad; hookah lounges in Serbia follow Serbian rules.

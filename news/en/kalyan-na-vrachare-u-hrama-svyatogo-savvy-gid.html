Hookah in Vračar: a hookah lounge in Belgrade near the Temple of Saint Sava

25 November 2026

Vračar is Belgrade's smallest and most densely populated municipality, and the Temple of Saint Sava is one of the city's main sights. Here is how to combine a walk around the area with an evening of shisha.

Vračar: a small district in the city centre

Vračar is a central Belgrade municipality. According to Wikipedia, it covers just 2.87 km², making it the smallest municipality in the city and in Serbia, yet also the most densely populated: the 2022 census counted about 55,000 residents. Terazije, the historic centre, is less than a kilometre away, so it is an easy walk from the Old Town.

The Tourist Organization of Belgrade calls Vračar's kafanas and cafés the district's best symbol, and the area has long had a bohemian, literary reputation. It is also home to Kalenić, one of the city's oldest and most popular open-air markets, while the streets around Slavija square bring a busier mix of bars and restaurants.

The Temple of Saint Sava: what to know

The temple stands on the Vračar plateau. The first stone was laid in 1935; construction stalled for decades because of the war and resumed only in the 1980s, and according to the Tourist Organization of Belgrade the exterior was completed in 2004. It is the largest Orthodox church in Serbia and one of the largest in the world: about 78 metres tall to the top of the cross, with room for up to 7,000 people. Inside are roughly 12,000 m² of gold mosaics, and the crypt church of Tsar Lazar is open to visitors. The National Library of Serbia and the Karađorđe monument share the plateau, with Karađorđe's Park to the southwest.

Combining a walk with shisha

A simple plan for visitors: the temple, crypt and park by day, then Kalenić market or a Vračar café, and in the evening a hookah lounge in Belgrade nearby, with no need to cross the city. A few practical tips:

  • Book weekend evenings ahead. On Fridays and Saturdays good tables in the centre fill up early.
  • Mind the age rule. Tobacco products are not sold to minors in Serbia, and hookah lounges are for guests 18+ only.
  • Ask about the room. Under the 2010 law on protection from tobacco smoke, owners mark rooms up to 80 m² as smoking or non-smoking, and larger rooms must keep at least half of the space smoke-free.
  • Ask for a hookah made for you. A good hookah master picks tobacco and strength for your group instead of serving everyone the same bowl.

What this means for Belgrade visitors

Vračar's advantage is that sights, cafés and evening venues are a few minutes' walk from each other. For tourists and expats this makes it easy to spend a whole day in one neighbourhood, without taxis or hunting for a place across the Sava. Smoking rules in Vračar are the same as everywhere in Serbia; there are no extra local restrictions.

FAQ

Is there a hookah lounge near the Temple of Saint Sava?

Yes, Vračar has hookah lounges within walking distance of the temple; for example, Spički at Mačvanska 10 is about a 5-minute walk.

Can you smoke hookah in cafés in Vračar?

Yes, under the general Serbian rules: in rooms marked as smoking or in the smoking section of a larger room.

What is the minimum age for a hookah lounge in Belgrade?

Hookah lounges admit guests 18+ only, and tobacco products are not sold to minors in Serbia.

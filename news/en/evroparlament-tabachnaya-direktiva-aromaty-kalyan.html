European Parliament calls for limits on tobacco flavours: what it means for hookah in Belgrade and Serbia

20 November 2026

MEPs have asked the European Commission to update the rules for tobacco and nicotine without delay, including limits on flavours. Here is how this relates to hookah and whether anything changes for hookah lounges in Belgrade.

What the European Parliament decided

On 12 November 2026 at its plenary session the European Parliament adopted a report on the implementation of Europe's Beating Cancer Plan. It passed with 447 votes in favour, 66 against and 91 abstentions. Amendments that would have softened the tobacco wording were rejected.

In the report, MEPs ask the European Commission to present a revised Tobacco Products Directive (TPD), in force since 2014, without further delay. The new rules should cover all tobacco and nicotine products, including e-cigarettes, heated tobacco and nicotine pouches. The suggested measures include plain packaging, restrictions on flavours that appeal to young people, mandatory ingredient disclosure, age checks for online sales and assessment of new products before they reach the market. MEPs also want the tobacco advertising directive updated to cover social media and in-store displays.

It is worth being clear: this is an own-initiative report. It does not change existing law or create new obligations; it sets out the Parliament's position.

The new rules may be delayed

While MEPs push the Commission to hurry, the Commission itself has reportedly hit a snag in its internal review. According to EUNews, as cited by 2Firsts and Génération sans tabac, the Commission's Regulatory Scrutiny Board issued a negative opinion on the reform's impact assessment on 9 November. The reported concerns include the proportionality of the measures and the legal basis for EU-wide bans. The opinion has not been officially published.

Brussels Report writes that the Commission will no longer be able to present its proposal before the end of 2026 as planned. ECigIntelligence analysts expect details in early 2027, though that date may also change.

Where hookah comes in

Hookah tobacco is almost always flavoured, so any EU-wide flavour restrictions could potentially affect it too. However, neither the report nor the coverage of it mentions hookah (waterpipe) tobacco specifically. Whether new limits will apply to shisha blends, and in what form, will only become clear from the Commission's draft law, which does not exist yet.

What it means for Serbia and Belgrade

Serbia is not an EU member, and EU directives do not apply here directly. As a candidate country, Serbia gradually aligns its laws with EU rules, so the TPD reform is worth following. For now, though, nothing changes for hookah lounges in Belgrade or their guests: the 12 November vote affects neither Serbian rules nor the range of hookah tobacco.

FAQ

Will the EU ban flavoured hookah tobacco?

Not for now. The European Parliament only called for limits on flavours that appeal to young people. Whether this covers hookah tobacco will depend on the Commission's proposal, which is expected no earlier than 2027 by current estimates.

Does anything change for hookah lounges in Belgrade?

Not at the moment. Serbia is not an EU member, so EU directives do not apply here directly. As a candidate country, it gradually aligns its laws with EU rules.

Is the European Parliament report binding?

No. It is a non-binding resolution that states the Parliament's position but does not change existing rules.

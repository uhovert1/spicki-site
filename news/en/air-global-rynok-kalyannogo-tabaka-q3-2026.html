Hookah tobacco market: AIR Global grows 11%, Europe lags — what it means for Belgrade

15 November 2026

On 12 November, AIR Global, which describes itself as the global leader in flavored shisha molasses, reported its third-quarter results. Here is what the numbers say about Europe's hookah market and whether any of it matters for Belgrade.

What happened

Dubai-based AIR Global, whose shares have traded on Nasdaq since 2026, reported its third-quarter results on 12 November 2026. It is one of the few shisha tobacco makers that publishes its financials, so its report is a rare chance to look at the hookah market in numbers.

Quarterly revenue was $99.8 million, up 11.1% from $89.8 million a year earlier. Shipments of shisha tobacco rose 5.8%, and pricing and product mix added roughly another 5 percentage points. Adjusted EBITDA grew 7.7% to $30.9 million, although the margin slipped from 31.9% to 31.0%.

Why sales grew

The company says the growth largely reflects a recovery from supply-chain disruption in the first half of the year. According to Nicotine Insider, shipping problems around the Strait of Hormuz forced it to set up alternative routes and speed up changes to its manufacturing network. Chief executive Stuart Brazier said that "end-consumer demand remains resilient."

The nine-month picture is more modest: revenue rose 6% to $306.7 million even though shipments fell 4.6%. The company posted a net loss of $74.2 million for the period, which it attributes mainly to one-off costs tied to its stock market listing.

Europe is the weakest region

The Americas (+25.6% to $21.9 million) and the Middle East, Africa and Asia region (+9.4% to $64.1 million) grew fastest in the quarter. Europe rose 4.9% to $13.8 million, with adjusted EBITDA of just $0.9 million. European volumes increased 6.9%, but price and mix fell 1.8%.

The company calls Europe a challenging market: in its view, excise increases and weak enforcement are fuelling illicit products. Its chief financial officer said it is on track to open a factory in Romania in the first quarter of 2027.

Full-year 2026 guidance was reiterated: revenue growth of 4–6% in US dollars with broadly stable shipment volumes.

What it means for Serbia and Belgrade

Directly, nothing changes for Serbia. The report does not mention Serbia or the Balkans, and one company's quarterly numbers do not change the laws or rules for hookah lounges in Belgrade. The sources do not say whether the Romanian factory will have anything to do with supply to Serbia.

Still, the report is a useful indicator. It shows that hookah remains a widespread habit worldwide, and that the main risks to the legal European market are rising taxes and illicit tobacco.

Hookah in Serbia and the law: where you can smoke shisha in Belgrade and the fines

19 November 2026

Can you smoke hookah in a Belgrade café, and what is the penalty for breaking the rules? We explain Serbia's current law on protection from tobacco smoke and what it means for hookah lounges — based on the text of the law.

Which law applies

Smoking in public places in Serbia is governed by the Law on Protection of the Population from Exposure to Tobacco Smoke (Zakon o zaštiti stanovništva od izloženosti duvanskom dimu), published in the Official Gazette No. 30/2010. It has no rules written specifically for hookah lounges, but its definition of tobacco products (Article 2) expressly lists cut tobacco for water pipes. So hookah lounges in Belgrade and across Serbia follow the same general rules as other cafés and restaurants.

Where smoking is banned

The basic rule in Article 3: smoking is banned in every enclosed workplace and public space. The law treats a space as enclosed if it has a roof and walls and less than half of its outer walls are open. A full ban also covers health, education and social care facilities (including their yards), public transport and enclosed waiting areas.

The exception for venues: the 80 m² rule

For hospitality venues — cafés, bars and restaurants — the law makes an exception that depends on floor area:

  • Up to 80 m² (if the venue is not part of another smoke-free space): the owner decides whether the whole venue is smoking or non-smoking and marks this on the entrance door (Article 7).
  • Over 80 m²: at least 50% of the area must be non-smoking, placed next to the entrance, and each table or zone must carry a sign (Article 5).
  • A separate smoking room in larger venues is allowed only under strict conditions: a fully enclosed room with self-closing doors and its own ventilation venting outdoors, with no recirculation; no food or drinks may be served there (Article 6).

Who enforces it and the fines

The owner or manager must enforce the rules, display the required signs and name a contact for complaints (Articles 11–14). Sanitary and other inspectorates, as well as the communal police, carry out checks. Fines under Articles 27–28: 5,000 dinars on the spot for an individual, 500,000 to 1,000,000 dinars for a company, 30,000 to 50,000 for the responsible person in a company, and 300,000 to 500,000 dinars for a sole trader.

Is a full ban coming?

Back in 2023 the Ministry of Health announced amendments that would extend the ban to cafés and restaurants and to e-cigarettes. But as 021.rs and Insajder reported in May 2025, the draft was never adopted and smoking is still allowed in hospitality venues. The version of the law published on Paragraf.rs lists no amendments. Hookahs were not singled out in reports on the draft.

Next door the picture is different: in December 2025 the National Assembly of Republika Srpska passed a law banning smoking in enclosed public spaces; according to Federalna.ba, the ban has applied since 1 August 2026. According to Paragraf.ba, the bill also covered hospitality venues, dropping separate smoking rooms. Reports on that law do not mention hookahs specifically.

What it means for hookah guests in Belgrade

For guests nothing changes: hookah lounges in Belgrade operate under the 2010 law, and the rules in a given venue depend on its size and are shown on signs. If the rules change, we will cover it in this section. Spički hookah lounge is at Mačvanska 10 in Vračar and is open daily from 12:00; bookings at book.spickibg.com. Hookahs are for adult guests only.

FAQ

Can you smoke hookah in cafés in Belgrade?

Yes, where the venue allows it: the 2010 law permits smoking in hospitality venues with limits. Up to 80 m² the owner chooses one regime for the whole venue; above 80 m² at least half the area must be non-smoking.

What is the fine for smoking where it is banned in Serbia?

5,000 dinars on the spot for an individual. For a company, 500,000 to 1,000,000 dinars; for a sole trader, 300,000 to 500,000 dinars.

Will Serbia ban smoking in cafés and hookah lounges?

Amendments for a full ban were announced in 2023 but, according to 021.rs and Insajder, have not been adopted. In Republika Srpska, according to media reports, a ban on smoking in enclosed public spaces has applied since 1 August 2026.
